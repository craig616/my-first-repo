Design routes · Route 1 of 6

Index, set in type

Idea
A cookbook index you can cook from. Words do the work: titles, times and what you have, set like the back pages of a good book.
Video
No pictures while choosing. On the recipe, one quiet “Watch the video” line with a thumbnail-sized still; the video plays only when tapped.

Why this route

UX

Fridge: who’s eating and search sit at the bottom, within thumb reach. Every entry already says “have 8 of 10”, so the answer to “do we have it?” comes before any tap.

Propped up: cook mode goes dark and shows only the step at 33 px, timers you can read across the kitchen, and Back and Next buttons 84 px tall.

Sofa: the index scans like a book. With no pictures to load, it’s quick to scroll and easy on the eyes.

UI

Newsreader sets the titles and anything you read for pleasure. Jost does the working parts: labels, times and buttons. All numbers are tabular, so times line up down the right edge like page numbers.

Choosing and reading happen on daytime paper (#efe8de). Cook mode switches to the walnut palette, with the yolk colour saved for the step’s key time and the running timer. Thin rules do the job that photos and cards do on the other routes.

Content

The tone is quiet: “Something for tonight”, “Check”, “if you have it”. Missing facts are left out, not guessed at. A time that isn’t given shows as a dash, and the serving count says “not given”.

Where the cod’s amount would be, the amount column just says Check, with one italic line underneath and no warning colours. Swaps (“or salmon”) and optional items (“if you have it”) are set in the secondary colour next to the ingredient. Cross-references (“Tinned fish, see Broccolini beans”) work as they would in a printed index.

Risks

Without photos, dishes nobody has cooked yet may feel abstract, so appetite depends on the titles. At 500 recipes the index gets long, and headings and cross-references will need care to stay useful.

Switching from light to dark for cook mode could feel abrupt. The dash for missing times is honest but could look like a gap in the data.
