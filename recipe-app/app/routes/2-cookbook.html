Why this route

UX

Fridge: who’s eating and search sit together in the bottom dock, under the thumb. “Have 8 of 10” is a row of small dots you can read at a glance, and each ingredient carries a tick or an empty ring.

Propped up: cook mode keeps one step to a page in 31px serif, the timers stacked low and wide, and Back/Next as 76px slabs. Sofa: Discover reads like a book you leaf through, one feature and then quieter entries.

UI

Daytime paper (#efe8de) with walnut ink, a deliberate move away from the dark default so the photographs sit on the page as they would in print. Hairline rules, generous margins, nothing in cards.

Newsreader, a soft text serif with optical sizes, sets titles, headnotes and steps. Jost handles the working parts: amounts, labels, times, buttons. Yolk marks only the things that need action: Start cooking, Quick, and the timer that matters.

Content

Headnotes are cut to two or three sentences in a plain, warm voice. Ingredients are set as a book sets them: amount in a narrow column, the thing itself in serif. Grams, 180°C fan and hob numbers throughout.

“Needs checking” is a pencilled margin note, not a warning: it says what’s missing and what the video shows. Swaps, like salmon for cod, live in the same note.

Risks

Paper is bright in a dim kitchen at night; it needs a walnut evening edition. A big feature photo costs space, so Discover shows only one or two other recipes above the fold.

Not every reel has a good still, and low-resolution heroes will look soft at this size. Serif step text has to be tested at real distance, not just at 31px on screen.
