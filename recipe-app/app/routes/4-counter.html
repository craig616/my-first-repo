Why it works this way

UX

Fridge: every screen leads with “have 9 of 11”, and each ingredient row shows a filled tick or an empty ring, so the two missing things stand out at a glance. Search, Start cooking and Back/Next all live in the bottom third, under the thumb.

Propped up: cook mode is a few big tiles you can read from across the kitchen: the step in 30 px, timers in 46 px, and Back/Next 96 px tall. Sofa: joined cards scroll like a pile of index cards, with rails underneath for browsing.

UI

One column of joined cards, 4 px apart, with large soft corners, on dark walnut. Everything is the calm brown card colour except one yolk card per screen: tonight’s suggestion, the key timing fact, the timer that ends first.

Jost only, mostly in its regular weight. Photos are kept to the hero and small round thumbnails, so they read as food rather than adverts. The primary actions use pale cream, not yolk, so the yolk keeps its meaning.

Content

The header talks like a person (“Evening, Craig and Mary.”), and the yolk card answers the real question: what to cook, and when to start. Time facts become clock times (“Start by 6.30”), because that is what you plan around.

Needs checking is a small brass line under the cod (“No amount given; two pieces in the video”) and “Serves ?” in the facts, so it is honest but not alarming. Oven heat is “180°C fan” throughout.

Risks

The yolk card only works if the app picks well. A poor “Tonight?” suggestion makes the whole screen feel wrong, and it needs pantry data people keep up to date.

The recipe screen is long: the method and log sit below the fold, under the ingredients. One yolk card per screen is a rule people will want to break once there are three things that matter at once.
